Coqui Frog Acoustic Sensor

2024 · University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa IPM Lab

Edge Impulse Machine Learning, Arduino Nano 33 BLE, C++

Overview

The Coqui Frog Sensor is a project I assisted with in Dr. Mark Wright's lab in UH Manoa's College of Tropical Agriculture and Human Resources (CTAHR) working with my graduate student mentor Ali Miarkiani. The sensor is a field device that detects and counts invasive coqui frog calls to track their presence and estimated density in forests on the island of Hawaiʻi. Invasive Coqui frogs affect Hawaiʻi's insect life and the relationships between native insects and plants. Creating automated ways to monitor their populations like this may eventually allow us to keep a more accurate, constant eye on an invasive speciesʻ population and how they interact with the surrounding environment.

Methods

Data collection

More than five hours of coqui recordings yielded over 300 isolated coqui call samples. I also collected about 1,000 unrelated sound samples so that the sensor would learn to ignore non-coqui sounds. All samples were isolated and cut into separate samples in Audacity.

Model and software

I trained the classification model in Edge Impulse and wrote the counting logic in C++ which you can see here. The display counts up to 999 and shows "full" at 1,000, at which point the sensor is collected and reset.

Hardware

The sensor runs on an Arduino Nano 33 BLE. I prototyped the circuit on a breadboard, then soldered it to a perfboard. A three-digit display shows the call count. The housing was modeled in Rhinoceros 3D on a birdhouse design, so units can be placed high in trees or on the ground while the electronics stay protected from wet conditions.

My Role

  • Trained and evaluated the classification model in Edge Impulse
  • Soldered, wired, and programmed all Arduino units produced, and designed their 3D-modeled housings
